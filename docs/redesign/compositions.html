Why did CHANEL need two anomaly detectors?

An unusual amount and an unusual combination of categories are different problems. A transaction can look ordinary in one sense and still deserve a closer look in the other.

At CHANEL Europe, I worked with hierarchical accounting data in an SAP context. A single global threshold would miss amounts that were normal overall but unusual within their business context. The first detector looked for amount anomalies within that context.

The second detector addressed categorical combinations: an amount could be perfectly ordinary, yet its combination of attributes could be unexpected. Combining these signals produced ranked candidates with interpretable reasons for finance review.

The important boundary is that these were candidates for human investigation, not automatic judgments that a transaction was wrong. The public method note explains the approach; the underlying company data and source code remain confidential.
